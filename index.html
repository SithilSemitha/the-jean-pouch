    From your wardrobe to someone's bag

    Four steps take a pair of jeans from the back of your wardrobe to a finished pouch. Here is what happens at each one, and what we need from you.

    Donate denim Shop the pouches
    1. Your part

      You hand over denim

      Jeans, jackets, skirts, shorts, offcuts. Ripped knees and broken zips are fine. Drop it off, post it, or ask for a pickup.

      Takes about two minutes: fill in the donation form and choose drop-off, pickup or post.

    2. Our part

      We sort, wash and cut

      Every piece is washed, unpicked at the seams and cut into panels. Pockets, waistbands and hems get saved for details.

      Nothing usable is thrown away. Even zips and buttons are kept for reuse.

    3. Our part

      It gets sewn into something useful

      Panels are patched together, lined with cotton and stitched into pouches, totes and slings. No two come out the same.

      Each piece is lined with cotton and checked at the seams before it goes on sale.

    4. Your part

      You shop, the workshop keeps going

      Every sale pays for thread, lining and the hands that sew. What we cannot use is passed on for fibre recycling.

      Add what you like to your bag and place the order. We confirm delivery with you.

    What one pair of jeans becomes

    Every part of a pair of jeans has a job in the workshop.

    • LegsMain panels for totes, sleeves and pencil cases
    • Back pocketsThe front of a back-pocket pouch, rivets and all
    • WaistbandHandles and straps
    • Hems and seamsTrims, loops and zip pulls
    • Small offcutsCoin purses, or fibre recycling when too small to sew

    Before you donate

    • Wash and fully dry each piece
    • Empty the pockets
    • Leave zips, buttons and patches on
    • Put everything in one bag or box
    • No need to repair or iron anything

    Questions people ask

    Do you take torn or stained denim?

    Yes. We cut around damage, so ripped knees, broken zips and stains are all fine. We only turn away fabric that is wet or mouldy.

    Is there a minimum amount?

    No. One pair of jeans is enough. For large amounts, such as a school or office collection, message us first so we can plan.

    What happens to denim you cannot use?

    Pieces too small or too worn to sew are passed on for fibre recycling. Nothing from the workshop goes to landfill.

    Why does my pouch look different from the picture?

    Each one is cut from different jeans, so the shades and patch layout vary. The size, lining and build are the same.

    Can you make something from my own jeans?

    Yes. Choose Custom order on the Contact page and tell us what you have in mind.

        About The Jean Pouch

        The Jean Pouch is a small sewing workshop. We take in denim people no longer want, and turn it into things that get used every day. The shop funds the sewing; the donations supply the cloth.

        In numbers

          Denim is too good to throw away

          Jeans wear out at the knees and the seat. The rest of the cloth is still as strong as the day it was woven.

          Built to last
          Denim is a tightly woven cotton twill, made for work. That strength is what makes it right for a bag that gets used every day.
          Costly to make
          Growing the cotton, dyeing it and weaving it takes a great deal of water and energy. Reusing a pair that already exists spends none of it again.
          Better with age
          Fading, creases and old stitch lines give each panel its own character. We keep them in, so no two pouches match.

          What we hold to

          • Use what exists

            We do not buy new denim. Every panel comes from something donated.

          • Make it to last

            Strong seams, cotton lining, proper zips. A pouch should outlive the jeans it came from.

          • Stay small

            Everything is cut and sewn in one workshop, in small batches, by people you can talk to.
